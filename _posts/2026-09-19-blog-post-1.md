I found other academics' writing useful in navigating PhD life and academia in general. So I thought of putting together a list of them.

* Prof. Matt Might's [blog](https://matt.might.net/articles/) has a set of articles for graduate students. 
  * [The illustrated guide to a Ph.D.](https://matt.might.net/articles/phd-school-in-pictures/)
  * [The shape of your problem ](https://matt.might.net/articles/problem-shapes/)
  * [A thesis proposal is a contract](https://matt.might.net/articles/advice-for-phd-thesis-proposals/) 
  * [3 qualities of successful Ph.D. students: Perseverance, tenacity and cogency](https://matt.might.net/articles/successful-phd-students/)
  * [Productivity tips, tricks and hacks for academics (2015 edition)](https://matt.might.net/articles/productivity-tips-hints-hacks-tricks-for-grad-students-academics/)
  * [Reading for graduate students](https://matt.might.net/articles/books-papers-materials-for-graduate-students/)
  * [Academic martial arts: Defending your Ph.D. ](https://matt.might.net/articles/phd-defense-tips/)
  *[Academic job search advice](https://matt.might.net/articles/advice-for-academic-job-hunt/)
  *[HOWTO: Get tenure](https://matt.might.net/articles/tenure/#fnref:1)
  *[Tips for work-life balance](https://matt.might.net/articles/work-life-balance/)
  *[12 resolutions for grad students](https://matt.might.net/articles/grad-student-resolutions/)
* Prof.Cal Newport's [Study Hacks](https://calnewport.com/blog/) blog. His recent articles cover digital ethics and productivity. But the blog archives have a lot of articles from his PhD and pre-tenure days.  
  * [Focus Hard. In Reasonable Bursts. One Day at a Time.](https://calnewport.com/focus-hard-in-reasonable-bursts-one-day-at-a-time/)
  * [Some Thoughts On Grad School](https://calnewport.com/some-thoughts-on-grad-school/)
  * [Some More Thoughts on Grad School](https://calnewport.com/some-more-thoughts-on-grad-school/)
  * [You Know What You Write: The Textbook Method for Ultra-Learning](https://calnewport.com/you-know-what-you-write-the-textbook-method-for-ultra-learning/)
  * [How to Read Proofs Faster: A Summary of Useful Advice](https://calnewport.com/how-to-read-proofs-faster-a-summary-of-useful-advice/)
  * [Fixed-Schedule Productivity: How I Accomplish a Large Amount of Work in a Small Number of Work Hours](https://calnewport.com/fixed-schedule-productivity-how-i-accomplish-a-large-amount-of-work-in-a-small-number-of-work-hours/)
  * [Einstein Was Boring Before He Was Brilliant](https://calnewport.com/einstein-was-boring-before-he-was-brilliant/)
  * [How to Win a Nobel Prize: Notes from Richard Hamming’s Talk on Doing Great Research](https://calnewport.com/how-to-win-a-nobel-prize-notes-from-richard-hammings-talk-on-doing-great-research/)
* [How to Read a Paper](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs114/reading-keshav.pdf) is a classic of research methodology classes.
* [Richard Feynman’s Letter on What Problems to Solve](https://fs.blog/richard-feynman-what-problems-to-solve/) 
* [You and Your Research](https://www.cs.virginia.edu/~robins/YouAndYourResearch.html), another classic of research methodology classes.
